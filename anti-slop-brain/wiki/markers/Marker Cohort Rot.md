# Marker Cohort Rot

Every list in this folder has an expiry date, and the dates are shorter than
anyone using the lists expects. A marker is a measurement of a moment: a
particular set of models, trained on a particular corpus, tuned by a particular
procedure, read by a population whose own writing was different last year. All
four of those inputs move. The list does not move with them unless somebody
moves it.

This note is the decay model. It says what is rotting, how fast, what to do
about it on a schedule, and what to do when something rots faster than the
schedule.

## Half-life, not accuracy

The usual question about a marker is whether it is accurate. That is the wrong
first question, because accuracy is a snapshot and every marker here was
accurate on the day it was measured. The useful question is how long the
measurement stays true, which turns a marker from a fact into a perishable
good.

Nothing in this vault is exempt. The ledger sets the cadence at 30 days for
model-specific marker cohorts and detector claims, 90 days for corpus studies
and regulation, and before every release for any numeric claim. Those intervals
are the operational form of this note, and the rest of it explains why they are
that short.

## Three clocks

Decay comes from three independent directions, and they run at different
speeds. Confusing them produces the wrong repair.

**The supply clock: vendors change what models emit.** A marker can be
eliminated by a training decision as easily as created by one.
`freeburg-last-fingerprint` measured base Llama 3.1 8B at 0.49 em dashes per
1,000 words while the instruction-tuned version of the same model produced
0.00, and argues from 12 models across 5 providers and roughly 240,000 words
that the rate is a signature of a specific fine-tuning procedure rather than a
universal property of machine text. `wikipedia-signs-of-ai-writing` records the
same movement from the field side, noting that GPT-5.1 already suppresses the
character and that some newer models carry instructions against overusing
boldface. This clock ticks on release schedules, which is to say without
warning.

**The measurement clock: the model roster the study used stops existing.**
`wu-verbal-tics` reports a Verbal Tic Index across eight frontier models:
Gemini 3.1 Pro at 0.590 as the worst, GPT-5.4 at 0.411, Claude Opus 4.7 at
0.317, DeepSeek V3.2 at 0.295 as the best, over 160,000 responses. The ledger
records that the model roster differs between versions of the paper, so the
per-model numbers are cohort-specific by the authors' own account. A per-model
index is a statement about named artifacts, and named artifacts are retired.
The ranking may survive a generation. The numbers will not.

**The baseline clock: humans absorb the vocabulary.**
`yakura-spoken-convergence` analysed over 740,000 hours across 824,634 podcast
episodes and 20,000 academic YouTube channels, and found that words
preferentially generated by ChatGPT increased abruptly in spontaneous human
speech: delve up 48 percent, realm up 35 percent, and adept up 51 percent
within 18 months of release. A pre-registered experiment with 496 participants
confirmed entrenchment in active vocabulary. This is the slowest clock and the
most damaging, because it does not remove the marker. It moves the control
group underneath it, so the marker keeps firing while meaning progressively
less. See [[Distributional Convergence]] and [[The Moving Baseline Objection]].

## The dated cohorts

`wikipedia-signs-of-ai-writing` maintains its vocabulary list in dated cohorts
rather than as a single set, which is the practice this brain copies.

| Era | Generation | Characteristic vocabulary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2023 to mid-2024 | GPT-4 era | Additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate, interplay, key, landscape, meticulous, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant |
| Mid-2024 to mid-2025 | GPT-4o era | align with, bolstered, crucial, emphasizing, enhance, enduring, fostering, highlighting, pivotal, showcasing, underscore, vibrant |
| Mid-2025 onward | GPT-5 era | emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing, plus the notability and attribution vocabulary |
| Cross-cutting | Grok, as of 2026 | causal, empirical, correlate, and a continuing overuse of underscore |

Two things fall out of that table and both are checkable by reading it.

The intersection of the three dated cohorts is a single word: emphasizing. A
vocabulary list built in 2024 and never revisited retains one item of current
value out of nineteen. That is the decay rate, stated concretely, and it is
steeper than any intuition about "the list is getting a bit old".

The second is that delve, the most famous marker in public discourse, belongs
to the first cohort only, and is simultaneously the word `yakura-spoken-convergence`
measured rising 48 percent in human speech. The single most confidently cited
tell is both out of cohort and contaminated at the baseline. It also appears in
`wu-verbal-tics` as a top tic alongside Absolutely, That's a great question,
and It's important to note, which shows how a retired lexical marker persists
in a conversational register after leaving the prose one.

## What rots fastest

| Marker class | Driving clock | Expected durability | Refresh due |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per-model tic indices | measurement | one model generation | 2026-08-26 |
| Vocabulary cohorts | supply, then baseline | 12 to 18 months | 2026-08-26 |
| Em dash density | supply | already turning, GPT-5.1 suppresses it | 2026-08-26 for the fingerprint preprint, 2026-10-25 for the pre-registered prevalence study |
| Individual words in isolation | baseline | already contaminated, Tier 3 by default | with the cohort |
| Sycophantic phrasing | supply | tracks alignment training, moves per release | 2026-08-26 |
| Structural defects: vacuity, padding, unsupported claims | none of the three | indefinite | not scheduled |
| Vendor residue tokens | vendor renderer changes only | until a product ships a new citation layer | 2026-08-26 |

The em-dash row deserves a caveat against the general direction of this note.
`czuma-em-dash-prevalence` measured the trajectory rising, not falling: roughly
4 percent of medRxiv Discussion sections through 2023, 8.0 percent in 2024, and
20.3 percent in 2025, on 69,632 preprints with pre-registration on OSF as
HFT8C. Rot is not the same as decline. A marker can strengthen for years and
then be zeroed by one fine-tuning decision, and both movements invalidate a
frozen threshold equally. See [[The Em Dash]].

## The refresh procedure

1. Read the `refresh_due` field for every ledger source the note cites. Any
   date in the past blocks a release under the strict tier the domain string
   forces.
2. Re-fetch each living document. `wikipedia-signs-of-ai-writing` is a
   continuously edited community page; compare the current revision against the
   stored snapshot hash in `.raw/sources/` rather than against memory.
3. Diff the vocabulary cohorts. Record additions, removals, and any newly dated
   era as a new row. Never edit a historical row: an era that ended is a fact
   about the past.
4. Re-check every per-model figure against the current version of its paper.
   Where the roster has changed, demote the figure to a dated historical claim
   and cite it with its cohort attached.
5. Check for a new model generation released since the last audit. If one
   exists, treat every supply-clock marker as unverified until re-measured.
6. Move anything that failed steps 3 to 5 down a tier under [[Evidence Tiers]],
   and record the demotion with the superseded figure still visible, per
   [[Superseded Figures]].
7. Update `updated:` in the note frontmatter and the `last_verified` and
   `refresh_due` fields in the ledger entry. An audit that leaves the dates
   untouched did not happen.

## Trigger conditions for an unscheduled re-audit

The 30-day cadence is a floor. Any of the following starts an audit
immediately, regardless of when the last one ran.

- A major model release from any of the five providers named in the current
  cohort table, including a point release that changes output style.
- A published system prompt or model card that names a style behaviour this
  brain tracks, in either direction: suppression or encouragement.
- A new corpus study reporting a human baseline for any marker in this folder.
- Any figure in this vault appearing in an external document, which raises the
  cost of it being stale.
- A source's `refresh_due` passing, or its `living_doc` flag being set on an
  entry that has not been re-fetched this quarter.
- A reported false positive on human-written text where the flagged feature is
  in a cohort table. One report is enough to trigger a check.
- Withdrawal, correction, or version replacement of any cited paper. This is
  what happened to the widely quoted excess-vocabulary figures, whose 10 and 30
  percent numbers come from a superseded preprint version and must not be used.

## The false positives that rot manufactures

A stale marker list does not fail safely. It fails in a specific direction,
against a specific population, and the direction is worth naming.

1. **Absorbed vocabulary flags people.** Once delve, realm, and adept have
   risen in ordinary human speech, a list written before that rise flags human
   writing for containing words humans now use. The measured rises are 48, 35
   and 51 percent within 18 months.
2. **Formal register flags disproportionately.** Anyone who learned the
   register from written sources, including English language learners and
   people trained in academic writing, sits closer to the model distribution to
   begin with. See [[Detector Bias Against Language Learners]].
3. **Old markers flag old text.** Text written before 30 November 2022 cannot
   involve these systems at all, and a list applied without a date filter will
   still fire on it.
4. **The complementary false negative is invisible.** Current model output that
   avoids the retired cohort passes cleanly, so a stale list quietly stops
   working while continuing to produce findings. That combination, confident
   output and declining validity, is worse than no list.

The mitigation is not a better list. It is [[The Firewall]] rule 2: a marker
never fails a document on its own, it routes to a structural procedure, and the
procedure's artifact is the finding. A rotted marker that routes to a passing
test costs one wasted check. A rotted marker wired to an exit code costs
somebody an accusation.

## What does not rot

The structural procedures do not sit on any of the three clocks. A span that
deletes with no named loss carried no information in 2023 and carries none now.
A claim whose negation nobody would assert ruled nothing out under GPT-4 and
rules nothing out under whatever ships next. An attribution that resolves to no
source is unsupported independent of who failed to supply it. This is the
architectural payoff of [[Signs Are Not The Problem]]: the brain's convictions
come from procedures with a permanent shelf life, and the perishable material
in this folder is only ever used to decide where to point them.
